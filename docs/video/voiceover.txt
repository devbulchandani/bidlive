[0:00] BidLive is a live auction that runs inside a chat room. There is no bidding form and no second tab. The room where people ask about a lot is the room where its price moves.

[0:10] Inside the lot, one box does both jobs. Type a question and it is a message. Type a number, or tap a chip, and it is a bid. The composer never changes, and neither do you.

[0:22] Fifty rupees is the whole increment, so one tap is usually enough. The bid lands as a message, and everyone in the room sees the same card.

[0:31] The server decides what counts. A bid below the minimum is refused outright. A seller cannot bid on their own lot, and a bidder the auctioneer has muted is refused too.

[0:42] Arjun is in his own window. When he bids five hundred and fifty, her card turns to outbid in place, and she gets a push. The person who was leading is the only one who gets told.

[0:51] A bid can be withdrawn while it is not winning. The lead is recomputed by replaying the transcript rather than decrementing a counter, so the card and the record can never disagree.

[1:02] Now the auctioneer closes the lot with under half a minute left, and the countdown turns red. Watch the last few seconds.

[1:12] The bid lands at eight seconds, and the clock extends by thirty. Nobody wins by waiting for the buzzer. And the extension is not silent, it is a message the whole room can read.

[1:24] When the clock runs out the lot settles. Winner, final price, bid count. Everyone who bid and lost gets a direct message, instead of being left to guess.

[1:34] You are rarely watching one lot. The rail lists every auction you are in, each row carrying its own live countdown, so switching lots never means switching tabs.

[1:45] Every bid, message and settlement is stored in a Nexconn group channel. Ordered, offline safe, and kept for a hundred and eighty days. So the transcript is the ledger, and a restart can rebuild the auction from it.
